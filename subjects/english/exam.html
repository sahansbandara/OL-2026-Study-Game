📋 Exam structure

English Language has two papers, both written on the same day. Paper I runs 08:30–09:30 and Paper II 09:45–11:45. Everything is written on the question paper itself. Total: 100 marks.

The two papers 📘

Paper I · 40 marks1 hour · Tests 1–8
Paper II · 60 marks2 hours · Tests 9–16

All tests are compulsory. In Test 14 and Test 16 you choose one option. Marks at the end are rounded up to a whole number.

Big picture: Writing (35) and Reading (35) together are 70 marks. Tests 14, 15 and 16 alone are 33 marks. Spend your best energy there.

🎯 How the writing marks are given

These breakdowns come from the official marking schemes (2020–2025). Learn them: they tell you exactly what the examiner looks for.

TaskContentLanguageOrganizationMechanicsTotalWord-count rule
Test 6 notice / Test 8 paragraph23--540–50 / 50–60 words. Under 20 words: max 2 marks overall
Test 14 letter / chart332210About 100 words. Under 40 words: max 4 marks
Test 16 composition552315About 200 words. Under 50 words: max 5 marks
  • Content = all the guideline points, with enough facts. Copying the question or writing off-topic = 0.
  • Language = few errors, good vocabulary, sentences joined well, style that suits the form (letter, speech, story).
  • Organization = correct format and connected paragraphs. Speech needs a greeting and an ending; story needs a beginning and an ending.
  • Mechanics = spelling, punctuation, capital letters and clear handwriting.
  • Content 0 means no marks for language, organization or mechanics. Length matters too.
⚠️

Objective tests: spelling must be correct in gap-fills (capital letters are ignored). Test 3 and Test 11 give half a mark per gap, so check every gap.
