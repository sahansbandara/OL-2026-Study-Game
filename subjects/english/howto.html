✍️ How to answer

Marks come from doing the right thing for each test. Below is a formula for every question style, with the marking criteria and the word-count caps from the official schemes (2020–2025). Model answers marked ආදර්ශ are our own patterns, not official answers.

🔗 Tests 1, 2, 3: matching and gap-fill (15 marks)

Objective tests: 1 mark per answer (Test 3: ½ mark per gap). Spelling must be correct; capital letters are ignored.

Read the whole text→Look for clue words→Decide the word class→Fill / match→Re-read
  • Test 1: match each instruction or sign with a picture by keywords. Item 1 is an example (do not redo it).
  • Test 2: read the dialogue, notice or invitation first. Check the word before and after each gap (a verb, a noun, or an adjective?). Cross out each word you use.
  • Test 3: look at the picture. Gaps describe what you can see (neatly, standing, ladder, racks). 10–11 gaps, half a mark each.
❌ Loses marks
Writing comfortible or nots (spelling errors = no mark). Leaving a gap blank.
✅ Gets marks
Writing comfortable after “It is ___ to sit here” because an adjective is needed. Copy the word exactly from the bank.
💡

Trick: use each word from the bank once. If two words fit one gap, solve the other gaps first.

❌ Loses marks
“If he will come, I will go.” (will after if) · “She complain about the noise.” (no tense/agreement)
✅ Gets marks
“If he comes, I will go.” · “She complained about the noise.”
⚠️

Intransitive verbs (complain, arrive, happen) have no passive. Reported questions use statement order: asked what the name was, not what was the name.

📊 Test 5: information transfer table (5 marks)

Complete a table from a text. 1 mark per row. Copy exact words from the text; do not paraphrase.

Read the table headings first→Underline answers in the text→Copy short, exact answers
❌ Loses marks
Row “Life span”: they live for a long time, maybe (too long, not exact)
✅ Gets marks
Row “Life span”: 30–50 years (exact words/figures from the text)
💡

Look at the column headings to know what kind of answer fits (a number, a place, a colour, a month range).

📢 Test 6: notice or announcement (5 marks · 40–50 words)

Marking: Content 2 + Language 3. Content 2 = all required points (2022: 3 points, 2024: 4 points, 2025: 3 points). Content 1 = some points; content 0 = copied or irrelevant. Under 20 words → max 2 marks.

NOTICE heading→Who + what→When + where→Instruction / request→Writer + post

Past tasks: dengue clean-up (2020), lost pencil case (2021), children's home visit (2022), railway announcement (2023, start with Attention please…), plant distribution (2024), return library books (2025).

❌ Loses marks (9 words)
Book donation. Please bring books to school. Thank you.
No date, no place, no writer, far too short.
✅ Model, ආදර්ශ (46 words)
NOTICE
Book Donation Drive
The Library Society will hold a Book Donation Drive on 20 March from 8.00 a.m. to 1.00 p.m. Students may hand over story books, magazines and reference books in good condition at the school library. Please give them to Mrs. Silva, the librarian, before 20 March.
Secretary, Library Society
💡

Use the guideline points one after another and tick them off. Write full sentences, not notes. Count your words: 40–50.

📖 Tests 7 and 15: reading comprehension (13 marks)

Test 7 = 5 MCQs on a story (1 each). Test 15 = 8 marks: True/False (½ each), MCQs, reference, synonym, short answers.

Read once for the story→Read each question→Find the proof line→Choose the matching answer
Question typeHow to answer
True / FalseFind the line that proves it. If the text says the opposite = False.
Reference (“her”, “it”, “his”)The nearest noun before the word that fits the meaning (her = mother otter; his = Magellan's).
Synonym in contextReplace the word with each option and re-read (limbs = branches; survived = lasted).
Short answerCopy the exact sentence or phrase asked for. Do not add extra.
Title / themeThink of the whole text: “You are never too old to learn”.
❌ Loses marks
Choosing an option because it “sounds right” without checking the text.
✅ Gets marks
Underlining the proof line in the text, then choosing the option that matches it.

✏️ Test 8: paragraph (5 marks · 50–60 words)

Marking: Content 2 (a paragraph with at least 3 points) + Language 3. Choose 1 of 2 topics. Use the right tense (past for “the sportsmeet we had”). Under 20 words → max 2 marks.

Topic sentence→3 points / details→Closing sentence
❌ Loses marks
I like rice. It is good. I eat it. (Only 3 tiny sentences, 10 words, no detail.)
✅ Model, ආදර්ශ (54 words)
The food I like most
The food I like most is rice and curry. My mother cooks it every Sunday with fish, dhal and a spicy sambol. I love the smell as soon as I enter the house. It is tasty, healthy and cheap. Above all, we eat it together as a family, so it makes me feel happy.
💡

Aim for 5–6 sentences. Use linking words: and, because, so, above all.

🧩 Test 11: banked cloze (7 marks · ½ each)

14 gaps, 15 words in the bank, one is extra. Past extras: brothers (2020), shorter (2021), cage (2022), on (2024), ever (2025).

Read the text with no words→For each gap decide: verb / noun / preposition / linker→Pick from the bank→Cross out used words
💡

The last word left over is the extra one. Linkers (However, Furthermore, but, because) often come at the start of sentences; articles and prepositions (the, to, in, about) fill small gaps.

🗂️ Tests 9 and 13: word forms and matching (10 marks)

Test 9: underline the best word in brackets. Word forms: noun after the / a / of (friendship); adjective before a noun or after be (special); adverb modifies a verb (intelligently, probably); verb after to / modal (communicate). Watch homophones: two/to/too, wear/where, right/write, nose/knows, four/fore.

Test 13: match 5 extracts to headings / captions / adverts. Find the topic keywords: recipe = cooking steps; advert = discount, visit, price; notice = dates, suspension; exam guide = tips.

📈 Test 14b: chart description (10 marks · about 100 words)

Same marks as the letter (Content 3 + Language 3 + Organization 2 + Mechanics 2). Content 3 = the chart data interpreted correctly. Use the hint words printed under the chart: most, many, more than, equal, less than, least, higher, lower, youngest, oldest.

Introduce the chart→Highest→Compare the others→Lowest→Summary
✅ Model, ආදර්ශ (100 words) — for a bar chart “Favourite subjects of 100 Grade 11 students”: Science 30, Maths 25, English 20, ICT 15, Art 10.

The bar chart shows the favourite subjects of 100 Grade 11 students. Science is the most popular subject, chosen by 30 students. Mathematics comes next with 25 students, while 20 students like English best. ICT is liked by 15 students, which is less than English. Art is the least popular subject with only 10 students. Science is chosen by three times as many students as Art. Overall, most students prefer science subjects to the arts. Together, Science and Mathematics are chosen by more than half of the students, so these two subjects are clearly the favourites of the whole group.
⚠️

Use figures from the chart exactly. Never write your own opinion. Do not copy the hint list; use the words inside real sentences.

🏆 Test 16: composition (15 marks · about 200 words)

Marking: Content 5 + Language 5 + Organization 2 + Mechanics 3. Content 4–5 = all points with enough facts and length. Language 4–5 = almost no errors, good vocabulary, style that suits the form. Under 50 words → max 5 marks.

FormStructureMust have
ArticleCatchy title → introduction → paragraphs (problems / solutions / role of the public) → conclusionTitle; school-magazine style
EssayIntroduction → advantages → disadvantages → your view → conclusionChoose a side and justify (village vs city)
SpeechGreeting → purpose → points → persuade → thank youRespected principal, teachers and friends; ending
Story / dialogueUse the opening given → events in order → endingPast tense; dialogue with speaker names / inverted commas

Model speech, ආදර්ශ (190 words)

Why young people should do volunteer work
Respected principal, teachers and my dear friends,
Good morning to everyone. Today I would like to speak about why we, young people, should do volunteer work.
First, volunteering teaches us to care about others. When we help sick people, clean a village or teach small children, we understand how much help is needed. Second, it gives us skills. We learn to work in a team, to lead and to solve problems, and these skills will help us in our future jobs.
Third, it makes our country better. A clean beach, a planted tree or a repaired road helps everyone. Some people say we are too busy with studies. But a few hours a month is enough, and the happiness we get is worth much more.
So, my friends, let us join a society or a project this year. Let us give our time, our hands and our hearts. Remember, small acts of kindness can change a whole community. Our teachers and parents will also be proud of us, and younger children will follow our example. Together we can build a kinder school and a stronger country.
Thank you very much.

Model story, ආදර්ශ (195 words)

The lost wallet
It was a rainy Monday and I was walking to school with my umbrella. Near the bus stand, something brown lay on the wet road. I picked it up. It was a wallet, and inside there were money, a bus pass and a photograph of an old man.
I looked around, but nobody was near. My friend Nimal said, “Let us keep the money. Nobody will know.” I felt uneasy. “That would be wrong,” I answered. “Somebody must be looking for it.”
We found a name on the bus pass: Mr. Jayasena. On the way, we showed the wallet to our teacher. She phoned the number on a card inside the wallet, and within ten minutes an old man arrived, breathing hard. He had been searching everywhere.
“I had saved this money for my medicine,” he said, with tears in his eyes. He thanked us again and again and gave each of us a sweet.
The next day the principal praised us in the morning assembly, and our classmates clapped. That evening my mother hugged me and said she was proud of me. I learnt that honesty brings a peace that no money can buy.
❌ Loses marks
One long paragraph, no ending, many spelling mistakes, 90 words, copied the topic as the first sentence.
✅ Gets marks
A title, 4–5 clear paragraphs, a proper ending, about 200 words, a final check for spelling and full stops.
💡

Plan for 5 minutes: write 4–5 bullet points first (they become paragraphs). Read it again in the last 3 minutes for capital letters, full stops, commas, apostrophes and spelling.

✅ Mechanics checklist (every writing task)

  • Capital letter at the start of every sentence and for names.
  • Full stop, question mark or exclamation mark at the end.
  • Commas in lists; apostrophes (Teachers' Day, don't); inverted commas for speech.
  • Spell correctly: choose words you are sure of.
  • Clear handwriting: examiners cannot give marks to what they cannot read.
  • Do not copy the question or the guideline words as your answer.
