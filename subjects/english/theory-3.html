🔀 Conditionals — types 1, 2 and 3

TypeUsePattern2024 T12 style
1Real / likelyIf + present simple, will + VIf you do not practise, you will lose / won’t pass
2Unreal presentIf + past simple, would + VIf I had a car, I would go
3Impossible pastIf + had + V3, would have + V3If he had practised, he would have won
  • Either half can come first: I will help if you ask. (no comma when if is in the middle)
  • unless = if … not: Unless you hurry, you will be late.
  • Never use will after if (Type 1): ✗ If it will rain → ✓ If it rains.
💡

Test-clue: look at the verb already given in the other half. will → Type 1 (present in the if-part). would → Type 2 (past). would have → Type 3 (had + V3). The 2024 key was: will lose, had practised, would go, won’t pass, had.

🧪 Self-check: “If I (be) you, I (not/do) that.”

were, would not do — Type 2 (unreal; ‘were’ is accepted for all persons).

🎯 How the question comes: Test 12 (5 marks): a passage or sentences with gaps; you fill the correct verb form of the conditional. 2024 official paper used this.
Competency 6.8 · Test 4 (2024), Test 12 (2022) Frequent in exams

🔗 Conjunctions — joining ideas

WordJobExample
although / though / even thoughcontrast in ONE sentenceAlthough she was tired, she finished.
howevercontrast, starts a NEW sentence, then commaIt rained. However, we went.
in spite of / despitecontrast + noun / -ingIn spite of the rain, we went.

whileat the same timeI read while I wait.

⚠️

Never use “although … but” together. ✗ Although it rained, but we went. ✓ Although it rained, we went.

💡

Real keys: 2022 T12 → whenever, although, because, while, however. 2024 T4 → as, and, but, if, or, after.

🧪 Self-check: “He was ill. ____ , he came to school.”

However — a new sentence, so it needs a comma after it.
