  • Read the passage once for the main idea, then answer.
  • True/False: find the line that proves it. If the text says ‘many’ and the statement says ‘all’ → False.
  • Reference words (he, her, it, they, ‘His’): look at the nearest noun before the word that fits.
  • Synonym in context: put your answer into the sentence; it must keep the meaning.
  • Short answers: copy a short phrase or write a full short sentence from the passage.
💡

Official keys: 2020 sea otters T,F,T,F; 2022 Tom Sawyer T,F,F,T; 2024 Magellan T,F,T,F; 2025 Mother Tree T,F,F,T. (Patterns vary — always prove with the text.)

False — ‘many’ is not ‘all’.

🧠 Implied meaning, idioms and elliptical replies

  • Elliptical replies leave words out: A: I play cricket. B: So do I. (= I also play) / Neither do I. (negative).
  • Idioms: a piece of cake (very easy), break the ice (start talking), under the weather (ill).
  • Implied information: what the writer means but does not say: He took the cane and marched her out = she was punished (use evidence).
⚠️

Do not choose an answer just because it uses the same words as the passage. The right answer matches the meaning.

🧪 Self-check: A: “I can’t swim.” B: “___ can I.”

Neither (negative elliptical).
